Digital Audit and Machine Learning for Fraud Detection Undergraduate Thesis · Field: Accounting · Auditing · Fraud Detection · Machine Learning

Research Title

Analysis of Machine Learning Based Digital Auditing for Fraud Detection: An Empirical Study Using Simulated Digital Financial Transaction Data

Research Type: Undergraduate Thesis · Focus: Digital Audit, AI/ML, Fraud Detection & Continuous Auditing

Research Overview

This undergraduate thesis explores the potential application of machine learning in strengthening digital audit procedures for fraud detection. The research examines the limitations of conventional audit approaches, particularly sampling and transaction based testing, in responding to increasingly complex digital financial transactions.

The study focuses on the conceptual application of machine learning, particularly XGBoost, alongside simulated digital financial transaction data to examine how data driven approaches may support anomaly detection, fraud identification, and more continuous forms of audit monitoring.

Research Background

The rapid growth of digital financial transactions has created new challenges for auditors in identifying anomalies and potential fraud. Conventional audit procedures, including sampling and vouching, may face limitations when dealing with large transaction volumes and increasingly complex patterns of digital fraud.

This research explores how machine learning could complement digital auditing by supporting the analysis of larger transaction populations, identifying unusual patterns, and providing earlier signals for further audit investigation.

Research Focus

The research examined five key areas:

  • Limitations of conventional audit approaches in detecting digital fraud.
  • The conceptual potential of machine learning, particularly XGBoost, in supporting fraud detection.
  • The use and validity of simulated financial transaction data for fraud related analysis.
  • Ethical considerations surrounding the use of AI and machine learning in auditing.
  • The readiness and evolving role of auditors in technology supported audit environments.

Research Approach

The primary research approach was qualitative and literature based, supported by an examination of concepts, prior studies, industry references, auditing standards, and simulated financial transaction data. The research explored digital auditing concepts, fraud detection challenges, machine learning applications in financial data analysis, the conceptual role of XGBoost in fraud detection, simulated transaction data from the PaySim dataset, the relationship between AI supported analysis and auditor professional judgment, and ethical, data quality, and professional considerations in AI supported auditing.

Key Findings

01 Broader Transaction Coverage. Machine learning supported analysis has the potential to expand transaction monitoring beyond traditional sampling approaches and support the examination of larger transaction populations.

02 Machine Learning as an Audit Support Tool. Machine learning can support auditors by identifying unusual transaction patterns and prioritizing transactions that may require further investigation. However, analytical outputs should support, not replace, professional judgment.

03 Continuous Auditing Potential. Data driven technologies can support more continuous forms of transaction monitoring and enable earlier identification of potential fraud indicators.

04 Importance of Human Judgment. The research emphasizes that auditors remain responsible for evaluating evidence, interpreting analytical findings, and applying professional skepticism and ethical judgment.

05 Data and Model Governance. The effectiveness of AI and machine learning in auditing depends on reliable data, appropriate model evaluation, regular updates, and human validation of potentially false positive findings.

Research Implications

The research suggests that the future development of digital auditing may increasingly combine accounting and auditing knowledge with data analytics and machine learning capabilities. Rather than replacing auditors, these technologies can function as analytical tools that help auditors prioritize risk areas, investigate anomalies, and support more data driven audit procedures.

Keywords

Digital Audit, Machine Learning, XGBoost, Fraud Detection, Financial Transaction Analysis, AI in Auditing, Continuous Auditing, Audit Analytics, Professional Judgment, PaySim

Kaizen 5S in Digital Financial Management Essay 02 · Type: Opinion & Policy Essay

Building the Foundation for Integrity Driven Bureaucratic Governance Toward Indonesia 2045.

Focus: Digital Financial Management, Kaizen 5S, Financial Administration, Process Improvement, Data Governance

Overview

A policy and opinion essay examining the challenges of managing digital financial administration in Indonesia, particularly the continued reliance on manual document reconciliation despite ongoing bureaucratic digitalization. The essay proposes the application of the Kaizen 5S framework to improve financial processes, documentation, transparency, and data traceability.

Key Discussion

01 The Digital Financial Management Challenge. The essay highlights a paradox in bureaucratic digitalization: while digital systems continue to develop, financial document matching and reconciliation still rely heavily on manual processes. The central challenge is presented not simply as a lack of technology, but as the absence of a continuous improvement culture in financial administration.

02 Applying Kaizen 5S. The essay applies the five principles of Kaizen 5S to digital financial management:

  • Seiri, Sort: Remove transaction evidence that has exceeded its required archival retention period.
  • Seiton, Set in Order: Organize contracts and invoices within an integrated system.
  • Seiso, Shine: Validate transaction values and supporting information before payment.
  • Seiketsu, Standardize: Periodically review and update financial procedures and SOPs.
  • Shitsuke, Sustain: Establish consistent habits of recording, monitoring, and evaluation throughout the fiscal year.

03 Digital Financial Management Workflow. The proposed framework connects the financial administration process: Transaction Submission → Automated Validation → Dashboard Monitoring/Review → Exception Handling → Organized Archiving, reinforced by the principles of Kaizen 5S to promote continuous improvement and disciplined financial administration.

04 SWOT Analysis. The essay evaluates the implementation of Kaizen 5S through four dimensions:

  • Strengths: Practical application in daily financial processes; supports transparency and data traceability.
  • Weaknesses: Work culture may remain focused on formal compliance rather than process improvement; dependence on separate archives; limited understanding of Kaizen principles.
  • Opportunities: Increasing digital literacy among younger generations entering the bureaucracy; real time monitoring capabilities; momentum toward the Indonesia 2045 development vision.
  • Threats: Resistance to established working habits; symbolic digitalization without meaningful cultural change; potential overlap between multiple digital systems.

Core Perspective

Digital financial transformation requires more than digital systems. Sustainable improvement depends on disciplined processes, organized documentation, continuous evaluation, and a culture that treats financial administration as an ongoing improvement process.

Overview

A policy essay examining the challenges of digital bureaucratic transformation in Indonesia, with a focus on fragmented government data, administrative inefficiencies, and the need for continuous improvement and procedural discipline. Key Discussion

01 Digital Bureaucratic Reform. The essay argues that the challenge of Indonesia's digital transformation is not simply the availability of technology, but the need to strengthen work culture, procedural discipline, and continuous improvement. Fragmented data systems can create data silos, requiring citizens to repeatedly submit and verify similar information across different government institutions.

02 Learning from Japan's My Number System. The essay examines Japan's My Number System as a comparative reference for integrated digital administration, particularly its approach to connecting taxation, social security, and population related services. The analysis highlights two principles: Kaizen, continuous and incremental improvement of workflows and data management, and Shitsuke, disciplined and consistent adherence to standardized procedures.

03 Integrated Digital Administration. The proposed administrative flow applies an "input once, use across services" approach: Citizen Data Change → Single Input → Automated Validation → Cross-Service Synchronization, supported by continuous evaluation through Kaizen and disciplined data updating through Shitsuke.

04 SWOT Analysis. The essay evaluates the proposed integration of SPBE data through four dimensions:

  • Strengths: Existing use of digital applications by government personnel; availability of foundational citizen data; familiarity of younger civil servants with data dashboards.
  • Weaknesses: Repeated data entry across systems; lack of automatic synchronization when citizen information changes; parallel manual and digital processes.
  • Opportunities: "Input once, use across services" approach; real time monitoring; learning from Japan's integrated administrative model.
  • Threats: System fatigue caused by fragmented systems; resistance when digitalization is perceived as an additional workload; loss of trust caused by inconsistent data.

Core Perspective

Digital transformation is not only a technology challenge. Sustainable digital administration requires integrated data, continuous process improvement, and disciplined execution to build a more efficient and trustworthy public service system.
